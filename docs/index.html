Bridging the Reality Gap

While computer-assisted surgery systems require massive annotated datasets to generalize, prior benchmarks are heavily constrained by single-center sourcing and limited procedural variance.

Cataract-LMM establishes the largest, most structurally diverse foundation. 3,000 phacoemulsification videos acquired prospectively from expert and novice surgeons, deeply annotated to link spatial kinematics with objective clinical skill.

By integrating multi-layer annotations—temporal workflow, pixel-wise semantic parsing, continuous tracking geometries, and expert-adjudicated performance scores—we furnish the data necessary to train universal, multi-task models.

5 Multi-Task Architectures

Independently accessible, intricately linked annotation modules.

Task 1
150 Videos

Phase Recognition

Frame-wise temporal boundaries tracking 13 surgical phases. Captured stochastic workflow variations natively handled by Long-term dependency models like ASFormer.

Task 2
6,094 Frames

Instance Segmentation

Pixel-level polygons for 12 classes (10 instruments & 2 anatomies). Provided in both YOLO and COCO formats.

Task 3
170 Clips

Object Tracking

Spatiotemporal IDs and functional tip keypoints for 469k frames. Maps kinematic dynamics during Capsulorhexis.

Task 4
170 Ratings

Skill Assessment

Synchronized with Task 3. A 6-indicator rubric (GRASIS/ICO-OSCAR) rigorously adjudicated by expert surgeons.

Task 5
SSL & VLP

Raw Unannotated

The full 3,000-procedure corpus acting as a knowledge base for Self-Supervised and Zero-Shot feature extraction.

Bridging the Gap in Surgical AI

Cataract surgery remains the world's most frequently performed ophthalmic procedure. Despite advances in surgical AI, existing datasets are severely limited by small scale, single-center sourcing, and single-task annotations, restricting the development of robust, globally deployable systems.

Cataract-LMM resolves this by providing an unprecedented 3,000 complete surgeries across two distinct medical centers, featuring profound diversity in surgical techniques, pathologies, and recording hardware.

Our benchmark provides meticulously crafted annotations across four key pillars: phase recognition, instance segmentation, object tracking, and surgical skill assessment, enabling holistic multi-task learning paradigms for the next generation of surgical foundation models.

Methodology & Tooling

Rigorous, expert-driven annotation backed by advanced engineering.

SurgiNote Platform

Custom-developed platform enabling a precise "coarse-to-fine" protocol for temporal phase annotation, designed specifically for rapid review of microscopic video data by expert ophthalmologists.

Human-in-the-Loop AI

Semi-automated pipeline utilizing YOLO11-L pre-annotation and BoT-SORT logic for tracking, massively accelerating the workflow while ensuring rigorous expert resident verification of final box geometries.

Multi-Source Acquisition

Captured inherently distinct visual domains (Farabi & Noor Eye Hospitals) using distinct hardware (Haag-Streit HS vs. ZEISS ARTEVO 800) to explicitly benchmark domain generalization under lighting & sensor shifts.

The Four Pillars

Holistic annotations facilitating comprehensive multi-task surgical perception.

Task 1

Phase Recognition

150 full procedures annotated into 13 distinct surgical phases using SurgiNote, providing complex temporal structures.

assets/task-1-phase.png
Task 2

Instance Segmentation

6,094 precisely annotated frames comprising 12 classes (2 anatomical structures, 10 instruments). Curated via Roboflow.

assets/task-2-seg.png
Task 3

Object Tracking (Kinematics)

170 continuous capsulorhexis clips (469,118 total frames). Features bounding boxes, functional keypoints (tool tips), and persistent temporal tracking IDs.

assets/task-3-track.png
Task 4

Skill Assessment

Directly linked to tracking data. Evaluates 170 clips across a rigorous 6-indicator rubric adapted from GRASIS and ICO-OSCAR standards (graded 1-5).
